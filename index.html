How to Work on This Academic Sequence

Welcome! This self-paced learning sequence is designed for 7th-grade students to close foundational gaps in the Present Perfect tense (regular and irregular past participles) and build step-by-step readiness for international B1/B1+ exams (Cambridge PET, IELTS, and FCE). Every module contrasts American English (US 🇺🇸) and British English (UK 🇬🇧) and requires zero oral speaking activities.

Step 1 • Visual Grammar Anchoring

The 3-Color Visual Code

Throughout all 4 modules, three consistent colors help you build accurate sentences without memorizing long rule lists:

1. Blue = Auxiliary Verb

have / has / haven't / hasn't

Matches the subject of your sentence:

  • • I / You / We / They → have / haven't
  • • He / She / It → has / hasn't
Example: Maya has visited London.
2. Green = Past Participle

Regular (-ed) & Irregular (3rd Form)

Always comes after the blue auxiliary:

  • • Regular: base + -ed (played, watched, visited)
  • • Irregular: 3rd column (eaten, seen, gone, broken)
Example: We have eaten sushi.
3. Red = Simple Past Trap

went, ate, saw, broke, did, wrote

NEVER put a 2nd-column Simple Past verb after have or has:

  • ❌ Wrong: He has went home.
  • ✅ Right: He has gone home.
⚠️ Watch for Red Traps in every module!
Step 2 • International English Standards

Dual Standard Guide: American (US 🇺🇸) vs. British (UK 🇬🇧) English

✓ Both US & UK forms earn 100% credit across all tasks
1. Spelling (-L vs -LL)

Single vs. Double "L"

🇺🇸 US: traveled

🇬🇧 UK: travelled

US keeps a single "l" when the final syllable is unstressed; UK doubles "ll".

2. Irregular Participles

get & learn

🇺🇸 US: gotten / learned

🇬🇧 UK: got / learnt

Note: learned is also acceptable in UK, while gotten is distinctly US.

3. Everyday Vocabulary

US 🇺🇸 ↔ UK 🇬🇧 Terms

• soccer (US) / football (UK)

• movie (US) / film (UK)

• cell phone (US) / mobile phone (UK)

• French fries (US) / chips (UK)

• vacation (US) / holiday (UK)

4. Grammar Contrast

just / already / yet

🇬🇧 UK & B1 Exams: Strictly use Present Perfect ("She has just eaten.").

🇺🇸 Casual US: Speakers sometimes use Past Simple ("She just ate"), though Present Perfect is standard in formal writing.
